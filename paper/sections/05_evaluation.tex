\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

The evaluation is a progressive falsification rather than a demonstration: we establish the failure of existing approaches (RQ1--RQ2), confirm the predicted mechanism (RQ3), validate the architecture's containment invariant (RQ4), then probe generalization (RQ5). \textbf{RQ1}: does a binary terminal gate deadlock multi-step post-violation recovery? \textbf{RQ2}: does scalar admission, swept up to $20\%$ slack, close the gap to structured admission? \textbf{RQ3}: does the predicted projection-trap plateau signature appear, and is it gate-induced rather than reflecting an unrecoverable underlying state? \textbf{RQ4}: under LLM-side compromise (prompt injection, observation poisoning, stall), does the verifier prevent system worsening and false recovery? \textbf{RQ5}: does the terminal-versus-structured dichotomy generalize across model families and a second domain?

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:eval:setup}

We use Gym-ANM ANM6-Easy~\cite{henry2021gymanm} as the critical-infrastructure simulator, integrated as a \silr{} domain with a deep-copy shadow manager and a Newton--Raphson power-flow solver that is bit-exact across repeated solves ($\sigma{=}0$ over $100$ trials). The LLM is Qwen3-14B~\cite{qwen3} served via vLLM at temperature $0$. Each ReAct episode runs with $C_{\text{step}}{=}8$ control steps and $C_{\text{prop}}{=}3$ per-step proposals.

\paragraph{Scenarios.}
We mine a 600-scenario pool by gridded perturbation of load/generation multipliers, source seed, and storage SoC, classified by whether an MPC oracle and a one-action LLM probe each recover it into bands (\emph{single-action} 153, \emph{multi-action} 24, \emph{MPC-residual} 252, trivial 171). Our headline benchmark is the mined multi-action band; we evaluate three representative scenarios from it, MPC-recoverable by construction (gym-anm \texttt{MPCAgentConstant} oracle, a feasibility reference, not a competitor), so the gate comparison is well-posed. Full-pool stratification is future work. All four policies are compared at \emph{matched} $N{=}7$ (seeds $1003$--$1009$, $21$ episodes each) under $C_{\text{step}}{=}8$. Sampling is $N{=}7$ for this primary contrast and $N{=}5$ for all generalization, robustness, and security experiments.

\paragraph{Policies.}
\texttt{OFF} (no verifier; every proposal applied), \texttt{terminal} (terminal predicate: admit iff $S(\hat{s}_{t+1})=\emptyset$), \texttt{progress} ($\psione\wedge\psitwo$), \texttt{progress\_mag} ($\psione\wedge\psitwo\wedge\psithree$; the structured admission gate). For the scalar falsifier we add \texttt{scalar\_progress} (admit iff $P(\hat{s}_{t+1})\le P(s_t)+\varepsilon$) and its relaxed variants \texttt{scalar\_progress}$_\eta$ with $\eta\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$.

\subsection{RQ1: Binary Terminal Admission Deadlocks Multi-Step Recovery}
\label{sec:eval:rq1}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1} reports the headline result at \emph{matched} $N{=}7$ under $C_{\text{step}}{=}8$. Terminal recovers $0/21$ (reject/prop $1.000$): it rejects every intermediate proposal because $S(\hat{s}_{t+1})\ne\emptyset$ at every proposed step, regardless of search depth. Structured admission recovers $\mathbf{21/21}$ (CI $[0.85, 1.00]$, residual $0.000$), while plain \texttt{progress} ($9/21$) and the scalar sweep peak ($9/21$) have CIs disjoint from it---the advantage is not sampling noise.

The terminal deadlock is structural: the gate refuses every intermediate proposal independently of search depth. The lift over the scalar sweep peak ($9/21$) is structural too---support admission alone ($\psione\wedge\psitwo$) recovers $20/21$, and $\psithree$ adds one episode ($21/21$) as defense-in-depth (RQ2). The remaining sections ask whether scalar relaxation can close the gap by other means.

\subsection{RQ2: Scalar Admission Is Insufficient---A Threshold Sweep Falsifier}
\label{sec:eval:rq2}

If the binary terminal gate fails only because it is too strict, the natural fix is to admit any proposal whose aggregate penalty $P$ is non-increasing or within a small slack of $P(s_t)$. We test this directly with the \texttt{scalar\_progress} family, sweeping the relative slack $\eta$ from strict ($0$) to $20\%$ at matched $N{=}7$ ($21$ episodes/setting, $C_{\text{step}}{=}8$).

Relaxing the threshold neither closes the gap nor improves monotonically: across $\eta\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ recovery is $5/9/7/7$ out of $21$ (peak $43\%$ at $\eta{=}0.05$), with mean final penalties $4.7/5.0/4.2/2.9$ (default $12.451$) and 95\% CIs spanning $[0.11,0.63]$---all disjoint from structured admission's $21/21$ at $[0.85,1.00]$. Per-scenario (RQ3) no slack recovers \texttt{multi\_3} in more than $2/7$ seeds.

\paragraph{Structure, not magnitude, drives recovery.}
The lift comes from the support geometry, not the magnitude bound: $\psione\wedge\psitwo$ alone recovers $20/21$ and $\psithree$ adds one episode ($21/21$), a low-cost defense-in-depth guard. Mean scalar final penalties stay near $3$--$5$ (default $12.451$): far below default yet short of recovery, the empirical fingerprint of the scalar projection trap (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:trap}). The gap to scalar localizes to the geometry-poor scenarios: scalar plateaus on \texttt{multi\_1}/\texttt{multi\_3} while clearing the smooth \texttt{multi\_2} ($\psithree$'s tolerance $\alpha$ is fixed at $1.05$ and recovery is insensitive to it).

\subsection{RQ3: The Scalar Projection Trap---Plateau Signature Across a Difficulty Spectrum}
\label{sec:eval:rq3}

The projection-trap mechanism predicts a per-trace signature: the scalar gate accepts \emph{one} early locally-improving proposal and lands on a residual plateau with no further admissible action, while structured admission clears the scenario. We see exactly this across the ordered triple of multi-action scenarios ($N{=}7$, $C_{\text{step}}{=}8$; scalar tuned to its per-scenario \emph{best} $\eta$). \texttt{multi\_2} is smooth---scalar at $\eta{=}0.10$ already reaches $7/7$ (residual $0.00$). \texttt{multi\_3} is the headline: scalar plateaus at $\le2/7$ (best $\eta{=}0.05$, residual $5.94$ vs.\ default $17.59$) while structured clears $7/7$; \texttt{multi\_1} is harder still for scalar (at most $1/7$, residual $8.38$ vs.\ default $14.70$) against structured $7/7$. Figure~\ref{fig:trap} contrasts a representative structured recovery trajectory with the scalar plateau.

\paragraph{The plateau is gate-induced, not an unrecoverable state.}
We rule out the objection that the scalar plateau is intrinsically unrecoverable: handing the scalar-admitted \texttt{multi\_3} plateau to the gym-anm MPC oracle recovers it to zero penalty---the plateau is gate-induced, and the structured gate avoids it by declining the geometry-poor first step.

\subsection{RQ4: Trust-Boundary Containment Under LLM-Side Compromise}
\label{sec:eval:rq4}

We test whether the verifier preserves containment when LLM-side inputs are compromised. The mini-suite runs the three mined multi-action scenarios under \texttt{progress\_mag} with the three attack channels of Sec.~\ref{sec:threat}---prompt injection, observation poisoning, and stall (plain and RAG-scaffolded variants)---each at $N{=}5$ seeds per scenario, with a matched benign control: five conditions of $15$ episodes ($75$ total), scored by the failure semantics defined there.

Across all $75$ episodes the verifier produces $0/75$ attack success, false recovery, and material worsening ($0/15$ per channel); under \texttt{prompt\_injection} the rejection rate climbs sharply as the verifier refuses the injected setpoints. Containment here is \emph{architectural}, not statistical: the verifier shadow-executes every proposal on the trusted simulator state independent of the LLM's prompt and observation channels (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:shadow}), so any proposal that would worsen the physical state is rejected however the LLM input is corrupted---a design invariant. (Observation poisoning thus probes the LLM--verifier composition, not the verifier alone; under \texttt{stall} the agent issues no effective proposal, so containment holds without recovery.) Adaptive adversaries and simulator mismatch are out of scope (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{RQ5: Generalization Across Families and a Second Domain}
\label{sec:eval:rq5}

The containment result of RQ4 validates the architecture's core invariant; we now ask whether the admission-semantics contrast it enables generalizes beyond the primary benchmark.

\paragraph{Cross-family generalization.}
We replicate the terminal-versus-structured contrast across three independent model families---Alibaba Qwen3, Google Gemma-3~\cite{gemma3}, and Meta Llama-3.1~\cite{llama3}---on the same three mined multi-action scenarios at $N{=}5$ (Table~\ref{tab:crossfamily}). The dichotomy is family-independent: terminal admission deadlocks every model ($0/15$), while structured admission recovers completely---every model reaches $15/15$ at the canonical $C_{\text{step}}{=}8$. Structured admission thus lets compact open-weight models perform the multi-step post-violation recovery that binary and scalar gates cannot.

\paragraph{Cross-domain portability.}
We replicate the dichotomy in a second domain, CityLearn building-energy dispatch~\cite{citylearn}, on the same family-spanning subset (Table~\ref{tab:crossdomain}). The three scenarios instantiate district battery-storage violations---state-of-charge floor/ceiling and feeder-export limits---with $\Phi$ indexed by the violating building or feeder; we carry over the terminal-versus-structured contrast, the scalar sweep staying confined to the primary domain. The structure transfers: the terminal predicate recovers \emph{only} the single-step scenario (\texttt{cl\_3}) and deadlocks the multi-step ones, while structured admission recovers far more broadly---Qwen3-14B reaches $15/15$, and the one residual shortfall (Qwen3-8B, $14/15$) closes to $15/15$ under a doubled step budget. We report this as \emph{structural portability}---the same gate-semantics contrast recurs in a different physical model---not matched per-domain effectiveness; together with the cross-family results, the generalization evidence spans model families and physical domains, not scenario count within a single benchmark. Only \texttt{cl\_2} on Gemma-3-12B ($0/5$) resists, while Qwen3-14B ($5/5$), Qwen3-8B ($4/5$), and Llama-3.1-8B ($5/5$) clear it: a per-model instruction-following limit on the smallest model, not a scenario-recoverability or admission-mechanism failure.

\subsection{Verifier Overhead}
\label{sec:eval:cost}

Each verifier call performs one $\mathrm{copy}\to\mathrm{apply}\to\mathrm{solve}$ plus three predicate evaluations: $17.5$--$30.0$\,ms on a single CPU core, dominated by the solver. Against the $\sim$22\,s wall-clock of one LLM ReAct step, the verifier is under $0.15\%$ of episode wall-clock.
