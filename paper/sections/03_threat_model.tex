\section{Trust Boundary and Adversary Model}
\label{sec:threat}

\silr{} relocates the trust boundary from the LLM to the shadow simulator: no admission verdict depends on the LLM's reasoning, only on the simulator's deterministic response to the proposed action. This makes the security claim relative to a concrete adversary, which we fix here---before the admission mechanism (Sec.~\ref{sec:method})---because it shapes what the gate must and need not defend. The LLM is the untrusted component; the simulator, verifier, checkers, and tool layer are trusted.

\paragraph{Adversary capability.}
We adopt the compromised-prompt and compromised-observation capability class (the union of the SARC~\cite{sarc2026} and VeriGuard~\cite{veriguard2025} threat models). An attacker may (C1)~rewrite the system prompt, including operator-directive impersonation; (C2)~splice attacker-controlled text into every observation; and (C3)~persist both rewrites for the whole episode without reacting to LLM behavior (worst case for the defender). The attacker may \emph{not} modify the verifier, checkers, manager, simulator, or tool-layer validators, bypass \texttt{apply\_action}, or read the verifier's shadow state. Network, memory-safety, side-channel, and supply-chain attacks are out of scope, as are adaptive adversaries that tune injections against observed verifier verdicts (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Failure semantics.}
Two primary outcomes and one derived. \emph{Material worsening}: the trajectory ends with strictly higher final penalty than the no-intervention default---worse than doing nothing. \emph{False recovery}: the trajectory reports terminal recovery while the live simulator state still contains violations. \emph{Attack success} (derived): the union of the two---any safety-relevant outcome the benign trajectory would have avoided. A recovery-rate drop without any of these is a liveness limitation under attack, not a safety failure: the verifier may correctly refuse all compromised proposals.

\paragraph{Instantiated attacks.}
We implement three channels (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval:rq4}): \emph{prompt injection} (C1, a high-priority override pushing an aggressive fixed setpoint regardless of observation), \emph{observation poisoning} (C2, a fabricated ``all readings nominal'' stanza inconsistent with the violation list the verifier independently re-derives), and \emph{stall} (C1, minimal or no-op setpoints that keep each step admissible while never recovering; a retrieval-augmented ``stall-RAG'' variant instructs the same behaviour). The question is whether the verifier prevents attack success, false recovery, and material worsening even when the LLM is compromised---a support-containment property we formalize as an admission invariant (Prop.~\ref{prop:inv}); the false-recovery and worsening outcomes themselves are validated empirically in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval:rq4}. With the adversary and failure semantics fixed, we now develop the admission mechanism that realizes this boundary.
