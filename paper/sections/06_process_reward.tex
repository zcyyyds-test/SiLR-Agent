\section{Internalizing the Geometry: The Verifier as a Process Reward}
\label{sec:reward}

The product order is not only the right admission criterion---it is also the right unit of structure for a policy to \emph{internalize}. We reuse the identical verifier---its graded verdict and the persisted branch state $\Phi$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:phi}---as a process reward for GRPO~\cite{deepseekmath2024grpo,lightman2024prm}, with no domain-specific reward engineering, and test whether it teaches the policy to recover on its own, so that recovery survives when the gate is removed.

\paragraph{Setup.}
We fine-tune Qwen3-8B with LoRA on the $9$ sub-saturated multi-action ANM scenarios (greedy decoding, without chain-of-thought). Three reward arms share the same structured rollout gate and differ only in the reward signal: \textbf{Geometric} rewards descent in the product order over $\Phi=(S,\sigma)$; \textbf{Count} rewards the count-delta of cleared violations; and a \textbf{Verdict-only} ablation strips the geometric component, leaving only the verdict ladder. We evaluate the checkpoint after the second GRPO iteration. Our primary outcome is \emph{ungated} recovery---the gate is removed at evaluation, so recovery measures what the policy internalized rather than what the gate enforces; we report it pooled over $5$ training seeds with Wilson 95\% CI, alongside gated recovery and steps-to-recover.

\paragraph{Geometry is the active ingredient.}
Table~\ref{tab:reward} shows a consistent ablation ordering: Geometric $>$ Verdict-only $>$ Count. The full geometric reward is the only arm that remains above the untrained base once the gate is removed ($0.844$ vs.\ $0.778$); stripping the geometry ($0.733$) or replacing it with a count ($0.667$) leaves the policy \emph{worse} than untrained---a reward that does not encode the geometry teaches reliance on the gate rather than internalized recovery. Geometric exceeds Verdict-only by $+0.11$ and Verdict-only exceeds Count by $+0.066$; the geometric reward also recovers fastest ($3.64$ vs.\ base $4.71$ steps).

\paragraph{Internalization generalizes.}
On $15$ held-out scenarios disjoint from training, the geometric policy recovers $14/15$ ungated against the untrained base's $9/15$ (one-sided Fisher exact $p{=}0.04$)---the policy carries the geometry to unseen instances, ruling out in-distribution memorization. The count policy matches the coarse held-out rate ($14/15$) but fails the mechanism check: its gated recovery falls to $0.80$, below base's $0.87$---it overfits the gate's support filter, whereas the geometric policy retains recovery when the gate is removed.

\paragraph{The multi-family failure class.}
When several physically incomparable violation families are active at once, any scalar reward must collapse the product order into one dimension. The representational gap of Prop.~\ref{prop:gap} then reappears in training dynamics as advantage collapse:

\begin{proposition}[Advantage collapse under scalarization]
\label{prop:collapse}
With $B$ concurrent violation branches, a count-based reward takes at most $B{+}1$ distinct values, so every action pair within a count-equivalence class receives identical immediate reward---no step-level advantage separation; and along a continuous partial reduction of any single branch the count reward is piecewise constant---zero gradient except at elimination points---whereas the graded geometric reward's severity term is strictly monotone in the reduction.
\end{proposition}
\noindent(Immediate from the definitions: the count delta ranges over $\{0,\dots,B\}$, and a partial reduction leaves $|S|$ unchanged. Empirically the flat fraction is $98\%$ of the continuous action range.)

We call a set of reward structures a \emph{failure class} for a training regime if every member fails to train recovery in that regime. To turn the structural deficit of Prop.~\ref{prop:collapse} into trained behavior, we run a controlled training-dynamics analysis following the specification-gaming probe methodology~\cite{krakovna2020specification}: two minimal multi-family recovery regimes that each isolate one component of the geometry, with GRPO on the unmodified reward functions. In the \emph{$\sigma$-heterogeneity regime} (one large-$\sigma$ family, one small-$\sigma$ family whose violation is urgent), the severity scalar is hijacked by raw magnitude---it learns to chase the large family and recovers $0\%$ of episodes---while per-family normalization makes the urgent family's elimination worth a full family and recovers $100\%$. In the \emph{coupling regime} (clearing the large family drifts the small family toward an irreversible limit), the count reward is blind to the drift its own actions cause and trains to $12\%$; the drift-aware geometric reward trains to $100\%$.

Table~\ref{tab:failureclass} completes the two-regime ablation: restoring the drift term rescues count in the coupling regime ($1.00$) but cannot restore cross-family priority ($0.80$ under $\sigma$-heterogeneity), and the severity scalar fails in both regimes. Scalarization itself loses the dimensions; each scalar augmentation addresses only one failure regime, and only the full geometry is robust in both. The sweep in Fig.~\ref{fig:trainenh}b shows the failure is not an artifact of a chosen operating point: it emerges at $\sigma_L/\sigma_S\!\approx\!4$, saturates from $8$ onward---CityLearn's measured heterogeneity is $286$---and vanishes in the homogeneous limit. The separation holds across the full $4{\times}4$ coupling--floor grid: geometric ${\ge}0.97$ in all $16$ cells, count ${\le}0.42$ in $15$; the single benign cell (coupling $1.0$, floor $3.5$) is the setting where the coupling is too weak to threaten the fragile family. The pattern is not specific to the policy class: an MLP policy reproduces the same ordering ($1.00$ / $0.13$ / $0.05$ for geometric / count / severity-scalar).

\paragraph{The mechanism at full scale.}
Training Qwen3-8B with GRPO on the multi-family CityLearn suite (four buildings, $\sigma$-het $286$) reproduces the mechanism in a second, larger system---and exposes a credit-assignment requirement of the multi-family setting. Trajectory-return credit is required to propagate delayed cross-family consequences: a per-step z-score cannot assign the cost of flooring the battery family back to the early step that caused it, so per-step normalization lets the count reward dominate locally despite global failure. With trajectory-return credit---the estimator under which the controlled analysis operates---the trained policies separate along the axis Prop.~\ref{prop:collapse} predicts.

Across seeds the full-scale experiments reproduce the mechanism fingerprints: the geometric reward's advantage signal tracks the true one-step action value at Spearman $\rho{=}0.96$ versus $0.79$ for count (GRPO advantages computed over the measured per-action rewards), and after training the count arm leaves the battery family at $14\%$ higher residual severity than the geometric arm at the same violation count---the signature of a severity-blind signal.
