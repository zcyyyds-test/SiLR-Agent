\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied runtime governance for LLM tool-calling agents under \emph{post-violation recovery admission} and identified the \emph{scalar projection trap}: a scalar gate, as a search operator over the ReAct stream, can commit the trajectory to a residual plateau. We support this with (i)~a representational impossibility (no scalar surrogate is sound w.r.t.\ the product order, Prop.~\ref{prop:gap}), (ii)~an LLM-independent admission invariant (Prop.~\ref{prop:inv}), and (iii)~an empirical falsifier: scalar slack peaks at $9/21$ while structured admission recovers $21/21$ (and $117/120$ on the full 24-scenario band) and terminal stalls at $0/21$, the plateau confirmed gate-induced by an MPC check. The dichotomy holds across three model families and reproduces on CityLearn (with recovery completion model-dependent); under a fixed compromised-LLM adversary containment holds across the security mini-suite ($0/75$), and a magnitude-redistribution attack that defeats scalar and support-only gates is contained only by the full per-branch predicate. Finally, reused as a GRPO process reward the same product-order geometry is the only signal under which the policy avoids regressing below the untrained baseline once the gate is removed, and it generalizes to held-out scenarios---an ablation isolating the geometry, not a scalar surrogate, as the active ingredient.
