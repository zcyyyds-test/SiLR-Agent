\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Deployment scope.}
\silr{} targets the medium-horizon advisory dispatch loop ($5$--$15$\,min cycles), not the sub-second AGC tick: LLM latency ($\sim$22\,s/step) precludes real-time control, and shadow execution corresponds to the power-flow study preceding each dispatch, with protective relays independent of the LLM layer.

\paragraph{Support-monotonicity.}
The admission invariant (Prop.~\ref{prop:inv}) assumes recovery needs no transient redistribution: $\psitwo$ forbids steps that overload a new branch, which holds for the Gym-ANM and CityLearn scenarios studied here.

\paragraph{Scope and design properties.}
The security claim is scoped to a \emph{fixed} compromised-LLM adversary on a trusted simulator (a corrupted model behind an intact control stack); adaptive adversaries and simulator--reality mismatch are out of scope.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The process-reward result of \S\ref{sec:reward} is established on a single violation family; the natural extension is benchmarks that concurrently activate multiple families, extending the product order across $S$, $\sigma$, and inter-family ordering---the regime in which a scalar surrogate must collapse incomparable violations and the geometric reward should separate most sharply.
